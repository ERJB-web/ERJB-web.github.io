# Connect a need, a context and a feasible change

Implementation involves work, relationships and resources. Define the practice separately from the strategy used to support it. Link a documented barrier to an action, its expected mechanism and observations. CFIR organises determinants; ERIC names strategies; FRAME documents adaptations. None automatically selects the best local solution.

## 1. Need and context

Work with the people concerned.

## 2. Strategy and mechanism

Explain how the action addresses the barrier.

## 3. Observation

Separate implementation, service effects and equity.

## 4. Adaptation

Record what changes and why.

## Fictional situation · proposed approach

A fictional CLSC and community partners seek to improve intersectoral referral. This teaching case is distinct from the thesis protocol.

### No coordination time

Reasoning : Document workload and negotiate protected time and explicit responsibilities.

Useful output : A working agreement, resources and monitoring of use, delays and displaced workload.

What to watch : Meeting counts measure activity; examine decisions and people’s experiences too.

### No referral feedback

Reasoning : Map information flows and jointly define a minimal feedback loop with clear sharing permissions.

Useful output : A pathway map, responsibility for follow-up and documented feedback.

What to watch : A referral sent does not establish service access or a need met.

### Unequal access

Reasoning : Examine who is missing and design alternatives with people facing language, disability, travel or digital barriers.

Useful output : Relevant reach and dropout measures, qualitative feedback and action on a priority barrier.

What to watch : Average improvement can conceal growing gaps. Protect confidentiality and small groups.
