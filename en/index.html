01 · Research

Doctoral thesis in progress · provisional topic

Organising the intersectoral response to complex and co-occurring social needs in primary care

Network configurations adapted to the different profiles of patients in care

How is the intersectoral response organised for the different profiles of primary care patients with complex and co-occurring social needs?

The project aims to link each needs profile to a configuration of partners and to its coordinator, at the scale of a CLSC territory (local community health and social services centre).

Exploration tableThe design in four steps

A scoping review

Identify recurring profiles of social needs from published studies, then turn them into four to six vignettes: validated fictional situations.

Scoping reviewVignettes

The CLSC ego network

For each vignette, the CLSC sits at the centre: we identify the partners it would involve and the ties between them. The result is one configuration per profile.

Network analysisEgo network

Interviews built on the network map

Interviews use each network map to explain the observed choices, for instance: why are these partners so loosely connected?

InterviewsMixed methods

A deliberative workshop

Patient partners and actors from primary care, community organisations, local governance, municipalities and the private sector draw up recommendations for each profile.

DeliberationPatient partners

Everything comes together in a joint display: one row per profile, with the network, the explanations and the recommendations.

Three concepts

Co-occurrence

At least two social needs present at the same time. It is counted: it is the inclusion criterion.

(Kreuter et al., 2021)

Complexity

The accumulation of chronic diseases, disabilities, mental health problems and social vulnerability. It is described, not required.

(Kuluski et al., 2017)

Intersectoral response

Actions by sectors other than health, carried out with it: CLSC, family medicine groups, hospital, pharmacies, community organisations, municipality, schools, childcare centres. It is organised within a territory.

(Leppo et al., 2013; MSSS, 2017)

Expected outcomes

  • For people: a response tailored to their combination of needs.
  • For the CLSC and its partners: guidance on whom to involve, how to connect and who coordinates.
  • For primary care policy: a baseline and recommendations aligned with the roles of the CLSC.
  • For research: a hypothesis linking the needs profile to the network.

02 · Approach

A pragmatic stance, mixed methods

From question to observation: each question calls for its own data, analytical method and interpretation. I choose methods according to what needs to be understood, drawing on several disciplines.

Pragmatic stance

A stance that combines a positivist reading (measuring, estimating, testing hypotheses) with a constructivist one (understanding the meaning actors give to their practices). The question determines the method.

Mixed methods

Integrated quantitative and qualitative analyses: network analysis, regression, thematic analysis, document analysis and mapping, brought together in joint displays.

Multidisciplinarity

Public health, law, management, political science and social intervention: perspectives that inform one another to analyse the same system.

05 · Biography

Each step grew out of the limits of the one before

Special needs educator, jurist and PhD student in public health: my path is anything but linear, and that is precisely what makes it coherent.

  1. 01

    Starting from experience

    I went through the child welfare system in France, then left school at 16. Neurodivergence taught me early on to learn differently, to find my own paths and not to give up. From that starting point I keep a conviction that still guides my work: a public policy should be judged by what it concretely changes in people’s lives.

  2. 02

    Music, then the field

    Studying came back through music, with a bachelor’s degree in musicology: listening, rigour and the perseverance of patient work. Alongside, youth and leisure programmes and then managing adapted stays for people with disabilities taught me to organise, to lead teams and to start from each person’s needs.

  3. 03

    Choosing social usefulness

    Then came a moment of questioning: what is my relationship to society, and how am I useful? I went back to school to become a special needs educator and to enter the health field through professional practice.

  4. 04

    From the limits of practice to management

    My educator’s thesis examined how educational practices shape self-determination. It showed me a limit: without the right organisation, even the best practice runs into the framework. So I turned to management, with a master’s in health care facility management and a university diploma in entrepreneurship.

  5. 05

    From law to governance

    Management in turn revealed its own limits: planning, law and funding are often decided higher up. I went on to Université Paris Panthéon-Assas, in public, social and health law, then to France’s national agency for performance support (ANAP), close to central organisation, planning, political science and governance.

  6. 06

    Researching in order to propose

    Today, the PhD in public health in Montréal brings these perspectives together: the field, management, law and governance. I am not only trying to understand; I want to innovate and propose better-organised responses for people whose needs cut across several sectors. The same drive leads me to entrepreneurship, with the creation of OWNNIGHT.

Per aspera ad astra: through hardships to the stars.
